# Development priorities after observing Experiment019

Ranked from the [actual observational investigation](history-explorer-observations-019.md)
at `bc15b455`, not from hypothetical community narratives. This is a small prospective
backlog, not approval of a policy formula or implementation of020. Foundation001–019,
all cognitive lifetimes and negative results remain protected.

## 1. Make resource/inquiry causal chains easier to inspect

**Category:** viewer engineering / observability. **Evidence:** C103 p0/s0→meal1→
consent1→scene0→e0/e1 at3/4; H7 p0/s3 planned inquiry loses to Resource2→0; H10
query0's native null coexists with provenance receipt1 and support20→60.

The original Explorer lets a reader inspect every stage but requires moving among
disclosures, time controls and separate namespaces. The initial “No response” label
was a correctness issue and has already been narrowed in isolated commit `a7ae325`.
It should not be counted as an unresolved simulation defect.

**Smallest next intervention:** add an observer-only typed chain showing selected
activity, actual recipient response, allocation, execution, delivery channel and
after-point, with original references. Include exact per-trial navigation links and
a derived zero-transfer scene filter that keeps Declined, Refuse and Leave separate.
Avoid a general provenance-graph redesign.

**Controls:** real C103/C107, H7/H10, empty query, redundant/wrong receipt, declined
invitation and missing actor input. **Evaluation:** a reader can identify the real
actor, delivery and before/after outcome without opening raw JSON; tests reject
invented links and demonstrate selected≠executed. **Constitution risk:** joining
observer chains into actor cognition or treating adjacency as sole cause. Keep all
new derivations observer-only. **Disposition:** tooling task; no behavioral experiment.

## 2. Study finite environmental access before adding more social intelligence

**Category:** new environmental capability / scientific research. **Evidence:** C85
point303 at257 still has one food; last meal258 creates all-zero point304. Scenes46–110
then complete65 Request/Leave encounters, transferring0; ledger36=0+36. The present
world offers no action capable of obtaining food outside private inventories.

This is the strongest next **behavioral** candidate because improving redistribution
alone cannot relieve need after every unit is consumed. A finite contested cache
could offer a non-social option, compete for time, change opportunities to share and
delay exhaustion without creating food. None of those behavioral effects is yet proven.

**Smallest prospective intervention:** one stationary, locally visible finite cache;
transfer at resolution with explicit cost, ownership, stock checks and failures.
Reuse the [scope proposal](resource-acquisition-scope.md), while recognizing its
old pre-protection019 status text is historical. Before any code, specify interaction
with contact arbitration, failed-attempt costs and local observation validity.
No production, farming, currency, travel or renewable supply.

**Required controls for a prospective020 recommendation:**

* Hold total potential food constant: compare initially agent-owned supply with
  the same units divided between agents and an accessible cache. Separately compare
  otherwise matched inaccessible-cache conditions; do not call additional supply
  an access-policy benefit.
* Include zero/limited/ample finite stock, late depletion, contested claims and
  opportunity-cost failures. Preserve losers and stranded supply. Use matched
  external opportunities until voluntary choices diverge.
* Fix a simple local selector and sensitivity levels before outcomes. Compare
  access/effort controls; do not retune mixed-unit scores to make acquisition win.
* Compare Ascending/Descending/Rotating arbitration and distinct need/attempt timing.
  Keep the recurring law, cognitive capacities and old modes fixed.
* Use local/private twins, stale observations and a source that can become useful
  again. A public object observation cannot reveal rivals' private inventory or
  future claim outcomes.

**Success/failure evaluation:** trace local observation→selection→actual attempt→
legitimate grant/miss→meal/contact→later persistent consequence. Conserve initial
agent+cache food = current agent+cache food + consumed, including every failed
attempt's elapsed need cost. Useful negative results include inert access, repeated
failed claims, missed cooperation, worse distribution or merely delayed exhaustion.
No global welfare sum or success target is preapproved.

**Constitution risks:** hidden future success, free replenishment, double grants,
private-state arbitration, simultaneous incompatible activities, scripted beneficiaries
and false sustainability claims. Exact replay, accounting and historical gates are
required. **Disposition:** recommended next behavioral study after a prospective
specification resolves these issues. Not implemented or started in this task.

## 3. Diagnose contact value separately from trust and observed yield

**Category:** behavioral scientific question. **Evidence:** C85 p4/s4 at101 chooses
Invite133 over Consume100 and gets food; p4/s8 at106 chooses188 and then gives food
back. At400, p16/s12, it remembers16 zero-valence Request/Departure episodes yet
selects Invite133 with trust100. A trusted partner is not an inventory forecast.

**Smallest intervention to investigate later:** first isolate existing local
information use, rather than adding a planner or erasing trust. Consider a pure
replaceable control based on already captured recent outcomes only after its
hypothesis and failure criteria are written. Existing016 Recent/Typed findings
must be reused; Typed remains unadopted. An encounter marked Met is not necessarily
a productive resource encounter, so contact feedback and resource yield differ.

**Controls:** unchanged ranking; paired sources that stay empty versus later become
useful; high trust with positive/zero recent yields; last-food scarcity versus extra
food; matched opportunity/matching order. Keep environment and supply fixed while
testing valuation. **Evaluation:** actual useful transfers/meals, missed opportunities,
wasted time and per-person pressure histories; fewer invitations alone is insufficient.
**Risk:** hindsight-based donor estimates, treating silence as disclosed motives,
confusing trust with truth, or changing old behavior to fit a new utility model.
**Disposition:** new controlled question, preferably after or explicitly orthogonal
to finite access. Current traces explain a mapping, not the best replacement.

## 4. Expose individual trajectories and legitimate missing context

**Category:** viewer engineering first; actor-observation capture only prospectively.
**Evidence:** C85 actor2 consumes12 units but executes no resource action despite110
invitations. C103/C107 redistribute meals2/6/0→4/4/0; final saturation hides earlier
differences. H182/H183 final Agents match while their consumption/event audits differ.

**Smallest intervention:** observer food/hunger/trust change timelines and per-actor
selected/accepted/executed counts, preserving exact timestamps, namespaces and missing
captures. A simple discontinuous plot is preferable to interpolating unrecorded
cognition. Later simulations may capture bounded actor-local assessment/progress
context at actual decisions, with immutable references and explicit ownership.

**Controls:** same-tick phases, complete null, temporary divergence/reconvergence,
evicted episodes with surviving trust, atomic inquiries without internal snapshots.
**Evaluation:** distinguish identical terminal material, identical final Agents and
identical final Snapshot; explain who was excluded and when. Measure added storage
before duplicating bounded state. **Risk:** retroactively joining observer audits
into past actor inputs; smoothing away gaps; calling pressure-time welfare.
**Disposition:** tooling improvement; any extra simulation capture requires its own
prospective observability/compatibility review, not rewriting archives.

## 5. Test opportunity geometry only where it changes reachable choices

**Category:** timing / scientific research; no established scheduler correctness defect.
**Evidence:** H49 excludes bound8 in window6 although C103's actual episode finishes4;
H183 selects0→1 but executes2→0; C85 Waits258–263 then invites at264; H139/C1 sampling
changes meal/pulse alignment under identical physics.

**Smallest intervention:** preregister a narrow comparison of admission/reservation
and physically grounded opportunities with unchanged policy and total horizon, rather
than replacing the scheduler or granting retrospective duration knowledge. It may be
useful to test acquisition under the existing geometry first, then isolate scheduling.

**Controls:** actual short/long encounters, empty and productive sources, all three
matching orders, aligned need phase, same supply, no rescheduling to favor a survivor.
**Evaluation:** admitted/declined/contended/executed counts, actual costs, meals and
persistent distribution, including nulls and reconvergence. **Risk:** future-aware
admission or preselected narrative outcomes. **Disposition:** new timing experiment
only when a specific prospective question warrants it; not an observational bug fix.

## Deferred possibilities and decision boundary

More independently chosen activities and game-facing daily lives are long-term
possibilities, not immediate engineering requirements established by this sample.
Adding factions, language, institutions, travel, production or a general planner
would multiply uncertainties before answering the finite-opportunity question.

No simulation correctness bug was identified in this investigation. The one proven
defect was the viewer's native-response wording and is fixed separately. Finite
acquisition ranks before a new cognitive system, but cannot alone explain or solve
repetitive contact, unequal access, score calibration or scheduling. The strongest
open question is whether a legitimately observed, costly alternative yields
meaningful voluntary allocation under existing heuristics, or merely relocates the
same repetitive behavior to another finite source.
