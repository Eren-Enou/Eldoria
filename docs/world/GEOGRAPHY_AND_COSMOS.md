# Geography and Cosmos

> **Document role:** Working authoritative source for the physical geography, astronomical structure, planetary environments, spatial relationships, and travel constraints of Eldoria.  
> **Canon status:** Highly incomplete. Several major locations and environmental principles are established, but the overall map of the cosmos has not yet been designed.  
> **Revision priority:** High.  
> **Scope:** Cosmic structure, worlds, oceans, celestial regions, major environmental domains, travel, spatial isolation, the Gate region, Celestial Fragments, and the geographical foundations of civilizations.

---

# 1. Purpose

This document records what is currently known about the physical setting of Eldoria.

Unlike `COSMOLOGY.md`, which describes the hidden nature of reality, this document concerns the world as a place:

- where civilizations live,
- how worlds relate spatially,
- what environments exist,
- how difficult travel is,
- where important resources and hazards occur,
- and how geography shapes history.

The current geographical model is incomplete.

No definitive:

- star map,
- planetary count,
- interplanetary distance scale,
- universal political map,
- orbital structure,
- or complete list of inhabited worlds

has yet been established.

Future development should avoid casually filling these gaps.

---

# 2. Geography Must Shape Civilization

## Established Design Principle

Geography should not merely provide scenery.

The physical environment should influence:

- settlement,
- food production,
- transportation,
- communication,
- warfare,
- political organization,
- family structure,
- trade,
- magical development,
- isolation,
- cultural identity,
- and historical conflict.

The major civilizations already imply very different relationships with geography.

### Oceanids

Belong fundamentally to particular planetary Oceans.

### Moon Clan

Possess a civilization capable of pursuing celestial objects and operating across multiple locations.

### Mudmen

Are strongly shaped by material scarcity and the availability of water and earth.

### Pirates

May inhabit unstable environments where permanent settlement is difficult.

These relationships should eventually be visible directly on the map.

---

# 3. Scale of the Setting

## Established Broadly

Eldoria is not confined to a single ordinary terrestrial kingdom or continent.

The setting contains multiple worlds or major celestial environments.

Civilizations can interact across astronomical distance.

The Oceanid civilization in particular exists across more than one Ocean world.

Moon activity also operates across multiple worlds.

---

## Not Yet Established

The exact scale is unresolved.

It has not yet been decided whether the principal story occupies:

- one solar system,
- several connected systems,
- a substantial region of a galaxy,
- an entire galaxy,
- or some other cosmological arrangement.

References to "galactic" scale in development discussions should therefore be treated as approximate rather than precise canon unless later formalized.

# 4. The Visible Cosmos

To ordinary inhabitants, the cosmos contains real:

- worlds,
- stars,
- oceans,
- celestial bodies,
- astronomical hazards,
- ancient structures,
- and traversable distances.

These are not merely painted backgrounds.

They possess physical consequences within simulated reality.

The fact that the universe is simulated does not make geography unreal to the people living inside it.

---

# 5. Simulated Astronomy

## Established Principle

The physical laws of the simulated universe may differ from external reality.

This allows the setting to contain:

- magic,
- unusual celestial structures,
- anomalous regions,
- and Gate-related effects

without requiring perfect correspondence with real-world astrophysics.

However, astronomy should still possess internal consistency.

The existence of simulation architecture should not become an excuse for arbitrary celestial behavior.

A world should matter socially.

People should not treat planets merely as interchangeable levels.

A person's home world may influence:

- language,
- food,
- architecture,
- religion,
- politics,
- body adaptation,
- magic,
- and cultural identity.

This principle is especially important for Oceanids.

# 9. Ocean Worlds

## Established

Multiple worlds contain significant Oceans associated with Oceanid civilization.

Each major Ocean is geographically and culturally important in its own right.

An Ocean is not merely:

> one branch of one universal Oceanid network.

Each planetary Ocean is its own strongest domain.

Oceanids are profoundly connected to their native Ocean.

---

# 10. The Ocean as Homeland

For Oceanids, an Ocean is simultaneously:

- habitat,
- homeland,
- sensory environment,
- defensive territory,
- cultural foundation,
- and part of personal identity.

Oceanids are born from or within their Ocean and generally expect to die there.

Leaving is therefore more consequential than ordinary migration.

---

# 11. Inter-Ocean Separation

## Established

Oceanids on different planets do not maintain a powerful natural water-mediated awareness of one another across space.

This geographical separation matters.

Different Oceans can therefore develop:

- distinct histories,
- practices,
- military traditions,
- interpretations,
- and perhaps dialects or cultural variants.

They remain recognizably part of a broader Oceanid civilization without functioning as one constant hive mind.

---

# 12. Ocean Depth as Geography

## Established

Oceanid geography is inherently three-dimensional.

For Oceanids:

- above,
- below,
- beside,
- near the surface,
- and deep beneath

are ordinary geographical relationships.

This should strongly affect settlement design.

Human-style flat maps will often be inadequate for describing Oceanid territory.

---

# 13. Ocean Depth Zones

## Established Broad Structure

Oceanid civilization contains a meaningful social and military relationship with depth.

The broad current structure is:

### Upper / Sunlit Waters

Associated more strongly with:

- ordinary civilian life,
- greater freedom,
- exploration,
- social activity,
- and vulnerability to outside contact.

### Intermediate Depths

Currently underdeveloped.

These likely contain much of the infrastructure linking ordinary society to deeper defensive regions.

No specific social structure is yet canon.

### Deep / Hadal Regions

Associated with:

- defense,
- military readiness,
- secrecy,
- sacrifice,
- and protection of the most sensitive regions.

The deepest territories are not simply privileged elite neighborhoods.

They carry the heaviest burden.

---

# 14. The Deepest Ocean

## Established Core

At least one Ocean contains the Prisoner's hidden confinement.

Its location is extraordinarily difficult to access.

The prison lies within or beneath extremely deep Ocean territory.

Exactly what "beneath" means remains unresolved.

Possibilities could eventually include:

- geological structures below the seafloor,
- tectonic cavities,
- magically isolated regions,

Multiple Ocean worlds must appear important enough that outside observers cannot trivially determine which one contains the Prisoner.

This means substantial Ocean defenses exist across many worlds.

Their existence should eventually have historical reasons beyond simply hiding the Prisoner.

A leading tentative explanation is that Ocean worlds already protected other dangerous or important sites before the Child was hidden.

# 16. Water Purity and Territorial Geography

## Established Cultural Principle

Oceanids believe Water should remain:

- protected,
- pure,
- and free from uncontrolled outsider intrusion.

This gives the physical boundary of an Ocean major political significance.

Crossing into an Ocean may not be analogous to merely crossing a national border.

It can represent entering something Oceanids experience as intimately connected to their collective existence.

# 50. Interplanetary Communication

## Partially Developed

Communication between distant worlds may not be equivalent to travel.

# 51. Communication Geography

If communication is substantially faster than physical travel, civilizations may maintain political relationships across distances that remain difficult to cross physically.

If communication is slow, distant regions may diverge much more strongly.

This decision will affect the entire setting.

It should not be treated as a technical afterthought.

# 54. Oceanid Territory

For Oceanids, territorial concepts likely center strongly on Water.

A planet's Ocean may be treated less like property and more like an inseparable homeland.

An outsider saying:

may misunderstand the Oceanid conception of the entire Ocean as connected domain.

This is a strong direction but should be developed in `OCEANIDS.md`.

# 69. Geography and Information

Where someone lives affects what they can know.

A remote Ocean colony should not instantly know what happened on another world unless a legitimate communication channel exists.

A Moon expedition may possess information unavailable at home until it returns or transmits it.

This principle directly parallels the World of Individuals simulation's Information Locality rule.

Geographical distance must eventually have informational consequences.

---

# 70. No Instant Universal Awareness

## Design Constraint

Unless a specific magical or technological system permits it, information should not travel instantly across the cosmos.

This applies to:

- wars,
- disasters,
- discoveries,
- political decisions,
- personal messages,
- and rumors.

The speed of information is part of geography.

---

# 71. Geography and the Main Plot

The major plot currently depends upon geography in several ways:

- the Prisoner must remain difficult to locate,
- multiple Ocean worlds must plausibly conceal her,
- Moon expeditions must have reasons to visit Ocean territory,
- Celestial Fragments must be geographically distributed,
- the Scar must narrow a previously broad search,
- and reaching the Gate must be difficult.

These needs are legitimate.

However, the map should not be built solely to satisfy them.

Every major region should eventually possess economic, historical, and cultural reasons for existing.

---

# 72. Avoid Plot-Shaped Geography

A warning for future development:

Do not create geography such that:

- every important planet contains a plot artifact,
- every civilization sits conveniently beside its enemy,
- every major route leads toward the Gate,
- or every ancient ruin relates to Life.

A functioning cosmos should contain large amounts of space and history that are irrelevant to the central narrative.

That irrelevance helps the world feel real.

---

- fashions,

- and different responses to the same civilization-wide values.

This will be important when moving from broad faction concepts to believable societies.

---

# 74. Planetary Scale vs Civilizational Scale

Do not assume:

> one planet = one culture.

A sufficiently populated world should contain internal regional variation.

Likewise:

> one species = one government

should not be assumed.

Some civilizations may maintain unusually strong unity for specific reasons, but that unity should require explanation.

Oceanid trust may support greater cohesion than ordinary human societies, but geography still creates different local experiences.

# 75. Ecological Development

## Major Missing Area

The current setting contains almost no developed ecology.

Future work must eventually consider:

- plants or analogous producers,
- food chains,
- predators,
- domesticated organisms,
- disease,
- agriculture or aquaculture,
- migration,
- environmental damage,
- and magical ecology.

This is particularly important for Ocean worlds.

An Ocean civilization cannot feel complete if the Ocean contains only Oceanids.

---

# 76. Ocean Ecology

## Undeveloped

Oceanids currently possess awareness of nearby creatures as part of their environmental perception.

This implies rich marine ecosystems.

Future development should determine:

- what Oceanids eat,
- whether they farm,
- what large organisms exist,
- whether domestication occurs,
- how deep ecosystems function,
- what threatens settlements,
- and how tectonic magic affects ecology.

No answers are currently canon.

---

# 77. Moon Ecology

## Undeveloped

It is unclear whether Moon settlements exist primarily:

- on planetary surfaces,
- in artificial habitats,
- in floating structures,
- or across several environment types.

Their food production and ecological relationships have not been established.

This must eventually be resolved.

# 78. Mudmen Ecology

## Undeveloped

Mudmen scarcity should emerge from actual ecological and material conditions.

Future development must identify:

- what resources are scarce,
- why they are scarce,
- what food systems exist,
- how water is obtained,

Without this, scarcity risks remaining purely thematic.

# 83. Geography and Class

Physical location may influence social position.

Oceanids already provide an unusual example where deeper location corresponds to greater duty rather than simple luxury.

Other civilizations may develop different relationships between geography and status.

Examples could include:

- proximity to ancient fragments,
- central academic districts,
- access to water,
- safe migration routes,
- or orbital position.

These should arise from practical conditions.

# 90. Contradictions and Risks to Monitor

## Too-Easy Travel

If travel between worlds becomes nearly instantaneous and inexpensive, Oceanid isolation and distinct planetary cultures become harder to justify.

Travel mechanics must support the social world.

---

## Too-Difficult Travel

If interplanetary travel is extremely rare and slow, large-scale Moon expeditions and recurring Ocean–Moon conflict become harder to sustain.

A workable middle ground or specialized asymmetry may be necessary.

---

## Universal Moon Mobility

If Moon forces can reach any world effortlessly, the reason Oceanid defense remains effective must be extremely strong.

---

## Ocean Worlds Existing Only as Decoys

Each defended Ocean needs genuine history and importance.

They cannot all exist simply to confuse the End about which one contains the Prisoner.

---

## Fragments Everywhere

Celestial Fragments should be important without becoming so common that every significant location possesses one.

Scarcity affects their historical value.

---

## Gate Geography Becoming Convenient

The Gate should not happen to be easy to reach merely because the climax needs characters to arrive there.

The entire story should respect whatever travel difficulty is eventually established.

---

## One-World-One-Culture Simplification

Planetary geography should eventually contain internal diversity.

Whole worlds should not automatically function as single cities with one personality.

---

## Cosmic Scale Without Logistics

Large spatial scale is impressive but creates obligations.

If civilizations span many worlds, the setting must eventually explain:

- communication,
- travel,
- administration,
- trade,
- defense,
- and cultural divergence.

Do not expand scale without accepting those consequences.

# 91. Development Priority

This document should be revised heavily after the major civilizations receive more ordinary historical development.

The next useful geographical questions are probably not:

> Where exactly is every planet?

Instead they are:

1. What does an ordinary Oceanid world physically look like?
2. Where do Oceanids actually live within it?
3. What does a major Moon settlement physically look like?
4. What allows Moon interplanetary expeditions?
5. What environmental conditions created Mudmen scarcity?
6. What resources cause people to travel between worlds?
7. How difficult is communication?
8. How difficult is physical travel?
9. What geographic conditions produced the first Ocean–Moon conflict?
10. What parts of the cosmos exist completely independently of the main plot?

Once those questions have answers, a map will become much more meaningful.

---

# 92. Core Geographic Principle

The geography of Eldoria should not be designed as a backdrop for events.

It should help cause those events.

An Oceanid should think differently because they grew up inside a living three-dimensional Ocean.

A Moon scholar should understand distance differently because their civilization searches across celestial space for pieces of a lost past.

A Mudman should value resources differently because the ground and water around them determine what they can build tomorrow.

A Queen's isolation should mean something because another Queen may be separated by an entire world and an enormous stretch of space.

The Prison should be difficult to reach because the geography, history, magic, and defensive civilization surrounding it all make reaching it difficult.

The cosmos should eventually feel less like a collection of places invented for the story and more like the physical environment from which the story inevitably grew.
