# Experiment 022 results

**ACCEPT WITH QUALIFICATION.** Four individuals execute disjoint resource scenes
and other physical work under one clock, with independent consent, owned inputs,
conserved material and persistent consequences. This establishes a bounded
scheduling capability in controlled fixtures. It does not establish a naturally
arising community, a universally better capacity setting or default adoption.

The protected foundation remains **001–021**. No 023 was begun. Original evidence
is [100 full histories](trials.jsonl.gz), [manifest](archive-manifest.json),
[source freeze](source-freeze.json), [reports](reports.json.gz),
[comparisons](comparisons.json.gz) and [causal slices](causal-slices.json.gz).
[Coverage](coverage.json) is a complementary post-evaluation audit, not new
simulation outcomes. [Verification](verification.md) distinguishes its checks
from the original archive's exact replay.

## Question, intervention and interpretation

Can four independent individuals conduct two disjoint social interactions and
legitimate environmental work concurrently while preserving local knowledge,
consent, conservation and historical causality? The primary intervention is one
versus two simultaneous social commitments. Both worlds have the same four
people, actual bootstrap history, physical starting conditions, profiles, seed,
passive observation cadence, need law and public opportunity horizon.

There are 100 registered rows, 50 matched capacity comparisons: 36 primary rows
and 64 parameter-sensitivity rows. The grid was committed before implementation.
No retention, inquiry, resource or recipient scores were retuned. The subsequent
implementation corrections and exact phase refinement are described in
[implementation notes](implementation-notes.md).

Some labeled families intentionally have equivalent conditions. Ignoring only
the case label leaves **84 distinct full histories**, or 42 distinct matched
configuration pairs. Thus neither 100 runs, seed replication, nor the frequencies
below constitute independent statistical evidence or estimated social probabilities.
The variations are fixed-grid sensitivity, not external transfer or a blind
confirmatory hold-out after implementation fixes.

## Demonstrated execution

| Observation | Original evidence |
|---|---:|
| Concurrent rows reaching two simultaneous social scenes | 41 / 50 |
| Matched pairs with observed interim food-ledger differences | 24 / 50 |
| Pairs with different terminal food ledger | 15 / 50 |
| Pairs with different terminal hunger | 20 / 50 |
| Pairs with different normalized terminal directed trust | 31 / 50 |
| Complete histories identical except the capacity setting | 9 / 50 |

“Food ledger” means owned food, cache stock and cumulative actual consumption.
It excludes hunger. Five pairs reconverge on that ledger while terminal hunger
still differs. Equal terminal trust also does not imply equal episodes, concerns,
protocol context or executed history. Complete nulls are the stronger comparison
of the whole recorded trial after removing only the capacity value.

Across all rows there are 5,065 selected invitations, 4,362 accepted recipient
reply records, 1,884 executed resource scenes, 803 transferred-unit events,
1,081 zero-transfer scenes, 235 refusals, 591 meals, 218 cache grants and 126
full-cost depleted claims. Transfers can circulate the same material repeatedly;
803 is not unique food created or a welfare measure. A stricter retrospective
availability test finds **44 sole-received-unit → actual-meal chains**: the
receiver had zero food before the transfer, and its next owned-food change was
consumption of that unit. Even these chains do not establish a global preference
for the transfer over every alternative use.

The coverage audit finds 204 resource/claim interval overlaps, 151 resource/meal
overlaps, and 92 world ticks with a resource scene, claim and meal occupying all
four people. It also finds 92 overlapping resource pairs completing together and
104 pairs with different finish times. These are interval-pair/tick counts, not
counts of independent worlds. Ninety-nine overlapping pairs contain one agreement
and one non-agreement; consent does not guarantee resource agreement.

## Inquiry: three different observations

There are **12 executed queries**, with legitimate retained support/status
progress from the prior inspection channel. A response or progress is not itself
a feeding benefit or calibrated inquiry value.

There are **31 accepted-protocol inquiry admissions rejected as AtomicBlocked**.
All are blocked by remaining protocol deadlines, not an already active physical
resource scene/claim at admission. In quiet trial 18 the first query executes
6–19. In conflict trial 20 it is rejected at 6, 8 and 10, then admitted at 12.
This is a fixture coverage contrast, not a capacity effect or a factorial isolation
of traffic. Failed admission does not deliver evidence or train inquiry learning.

Separately, **52 local query candidates are masked before invitation**: 26 during
resource work, 25 during replies, and one during a live claim. These are not spent
queries. In trial **13**, tick **14**, actor 3 has a Direct candidate about concern
0 from source 0, but the exclusive lane is unavailable while actor 2's claim **16**
is live until tick **15**. Actor 3 instead selects Acquire. Claim 16 resolves at
15; no atomic jump skips it. This exercises conservative pre-proposal masking;
it does not demonstrate that a consented inquiry would otherwise have crossed
that deadline. The handshake itself also costs time. True concurrent inquiries
and inquiry/physical-work interleaving remain explicitly unsupported.

## Costs, negative results and nulls

The primary `paired` comparison 0/1 has 13→18 resource scenes but 13→9 transfer
events and 9→8 meals. Concurrent-minus-serial hunger areas are
**[-840,+45,-1350,+100]** for actors 0–3: benefits and costs are distributed
differently. Hunger area is the sum of each actor's pre-advance hunger over elapsed
ticks, not utility. The `mixed` pair 2/3 has 13→16 scenes, 9→8 meals and area
deltas **[+240,+80,0,0]**. Extra capacity is not automatically useful.

In `durations` 4/5 the food ledger reconverges but hunger areas and terminal hunger
do not. In `busy_recipient` 10/11 the same initial acquisition makes a selected
contact unavailable; actual sender/reply costs and later consumption persist.
The record supports a missed selected contact, not proof that an unexecuted gift
would have succeeded or been globally preferable. Material results can match
while trust differs.

Long `ongoing` 32/33 has 57→86 scenes, 12 meals each, and lower cumulative hunger
for every concurrent actor: deltas **[-2360,-2460,-2280,-3220]**. Both consume all
12 available units. Nevertheless terminal hunger is **[70,5,25,65]** versus
**[70,65,100,100]**; food-ledger reconvergence is not physiological reconvergence.
Meal timing and the public closing budget matter. Exhaustion 34/35 has area
deltas **[+420,0,+2400,-430]**, again mixed. Capacity creates no food.

Nine whole-trial capacity nulls include 18/19, 20/21 and 30/31. The `null` family
still executes zero-transfer contacts as need rises; “null” means a treatment
null, not an empty or inactive history.

Claim duration 2 in paired36/37 gives area deltas [-160,+80,+40,-25]. Duration 8
in 44/45 gives [0,-90,-1280,-270], **13→12 scenes but 6→12 meals**. Extra capacity
can yield fewer contacts and more consumption. Descending order 52/53 gives
[-1350,-1200,-100,+155], and Rotating60/61 gives [-405,-145,-1410,+225]. Ordering
redistributes opportunities and costs; it is not a fairness optimizer.

Paired horizons16 and96 retain different effects;96has all-negative cumulative
hunger deltas despite terminal-state qualifications. Inquiry-conflict duration
variants are complete capacity nulls: no cache acquisition occurs there, so a
changed claim duration need not matter. Seeds1/1729 preserve the primary paired
pattern; they are exact deterministic variations, not additional statistical trials.
No policy was changed between these variants.

## Cost and limits

The 100 histories occupy **83.76 MB raw /2.29 MB compressed**; the largest member
is 4.18 MB. The streamed 100-world run plus validation/JSONL writing took **0.49 s**
and peaked at **9.42 MB Windows working set** in the measured process. Twenty-four
sequential samples that additionally hold serialized byte vectors peaked at
**14.57 MB**. Sample medians were 1.3–1.6 ms for short runs and 6.3–7.0 ms for
192-tick ongoing runs; serialization was approximately 1.1–1.7 and 6.5–6.6 ms,
respectively. These workloads differ; this is not an isolated scheduler-memory
cost or a matched pre-change speed comparison. [Performance](performance.json)
keeps wall time outside deterministic evidence. Legacy throughput also ran.

Live reservations/tokens and cognition are bounded. Observer scenes, events,
transitions, boundaries, need, memory-change, claim and inquiry records grow with
history. Serialization can cost as much as run/validation in these samples. No
general audit refactor or optimization was performed.

The fixture population is four, co-present under a public opportunity budget.
Prior pair preference comes from controlled executed bootstrap history; all
partners remain candidates. Positive overlap does not prove natural partner
formation. Long runs span 24 passive observation windows without resets, but do
not test reopening closed social budgets. Late affordability masks and observation
opportunity losses affect behavior. No institutions, travel, language generation,
planner, production, consciousness or broad social emergence is established.

The strongest remaining question is whether a finite, delayed material opportunity
would receive voluntary attention and lead to actual use under these contact
priorities and time constraints, rather than simply adding ignored stock or more
busy time. That requires a separately authorized prospective design; 023 is not
implemented or preregistered here.
