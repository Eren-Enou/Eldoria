## Performance and storage

Separate release recording/serialization samples took 0.9644, 1.2829 and 0.9120
seconds for the entire 56-world matrix (median about 58.1 worlds/second). Every
sample's decoded SHA-256 exactly matched the original; no archive was overwritten.
An exact replay took 1.583 seconds. The first registered process took 1.0604
seconds and peaked at 22,687,744 bytes Windows working set, sampled 196 times.
This is whole-process recording memory, not a measurement of deep live cognition.

The archive is 4,816,228 compressed bytes and 177,596,722 decoded bytes. Explicit
valuation audit rows occupy 3,752,089 serialized bytes (about 2.11% of decoded
output), referencing 8,668 captured decisions. Full physical/observer histories
remain the dominant cost: the two 768-horizon rows occupy about 18.93 and 26.80 MB
decoded. New audit terms add no live cognitive capacity. Histories still grow;
bounded cognition does not imply bounded observer storage.

The independent reader took 68.56 seconds in a separate measurement, with a
brief supplementary causal-slice extraction overlapping it. Treat that timing as
indicative rather than isolated. Matrix/replay samples ran sequentially while
other simulation/validation workloads were idle. There is no isolated old-host
versus-new-host speed benchmark and no causal speed advantage claim. Actual
samples and qualifications are in `performance.json` and
`performance-original-run.json`. Compilation, validation and simulation times are
distinct. No general optimization or audit-infrastructure refactor was performed.
